# Impact Moves — the engagement vocabulary and its density law

Director order 2026-09-04 (E02, d-085), built from viewer feedback on E01: audiences singled out **S05A** (the cookie-sled ride) and **S05B** (the frozen-fall orbit) as the moments they loved most, and engagement rose on them. This file is the study of WHY those two clips work, the vocabulary distilled from them, and the binding density rule for placing such moments across an episode. `skills/tech/seedance-prompting` holds the short rule; this file is the reference it points to.

The two source clips (E01, `projects/_series/micro-explorers/episodes/micro-explorers/assets/clips/`):
- `S05A_C01_std1080.mp4` — 15s, Higgsfield job bb1194da. One continuous take, rear chase lock, one speed ramp.
- `S05B_C01_std1080.mp4` — 4s, Higgsfield job b809f492. Inserted bridge, freeze-and-orbit, start AND end frame pinned.

Both were **Director-inserted scenes** (d-117, d-120) placed between two story beats — wordless, SFX-driven, pure spectacle. That placement is part of the lesson: the ride was not squeezed into a dialogue scene; it was given its own scene.

## What the two clips actually do (anatomy, from the frames and the prompts)

**S05A — the ride.** The prompt is a *beat ladder*: six named physical beats in one take, each a different relation between the riders and the world — PLUNGE (near-vertical, alongside the fall) → SKIM (inches over the pool, twin spray lines) → SWARM (rising into bubbles) → SLOW-MO BUBBLE (one head-sized bubble comes to the lens, time ramps down, the riders soften beyond it and appear curved in its skin, then it pops and time SNAPS back) → WEAVE (carving on edge between arches, strands swinging in the wake) → GAP (a razor-narrow gap, walls blurring inches from the lens, light dipping to plum and bursting back) → BURST-REVEAL (the flume ends, the camera cranes back, the whole cavern opens at once and the riders become a speck). The camera never leaves its rear chase position. The mount has *physics modes* (nose-down streamlined / carving on edge / catching air) and sheds crumbs on hard banks — mass, not floating. Sound stretches low through the slow-mo and snaps back with a surge. Voices are wordless whoops.

What the viewer feels: **speed with a held breath inside it, then scale.** Compression (gap) → release (reveal). One time trick, placed at the middle, never repeated.

**S05B — the held breath.** Four seconds: a real-time fall, then time STOPS — every droplet hangs as a crisp readable bead, expressions locked mid-whoop — and the camera alone moves, a slow half-orbit from behind through profile to three-quarter, the cavern wheeling behind; then time snaps back hard and they drop away to a pinned end frame (S06's first frame). Near-silence with a crystalline shimmer during the freeze; a whoosh surge on the snap.

What the viewer feels: **the world stops to let you look at the thing you were too fast to see.** The orbit gives the audience a gift the characters cannot have.

**The shared engine:** ONE named time/space event per clip · a named subject for it · an INSTANT return (never gradual) · particles crisp and individually readable · sound designed as a contrast (stretch/silence → snap/surge) · positive locks stating what must stay crisp · everything else in the clip is honest physics.

## The vocabulary (each is ONE named event; the prompt states what starts it, what ends it, what stays crisp)

Proven in production (d-120):
1. **Speed ramp** — real time → super slow motion on ONE named subject → instant snap back. Variant *ramp on a passing thing*: one bubble / birdsong ring / lantern-butterfly / gold spark sails up to the lens in slow motion, the riders soft beyond it, then the snap.
2. **Freeze-and-orbit** — time stops; the camera sweeps a smooth arc around suspended subjects through hanging particles; frozen expressions directed; snap back.
3. **Masked reframe** — a flash/whiteout/occlusion fills the frame and hides a full repositioning inside one continuous shot.
4. **Rear chase lock** — the camera rides the subject's tail, 0.5–0.8 m back, world rushing past; variant *vertical* (plunge or rise through a shaft, walls streaming).
5. **Motivated emotional drift** (d-118) — the push-in on an emotional landing. Everyday grammar, NOT counted against the impact budget.

Distilled from S05A/S05B, new to the vocabulary (E02 onward):
6. **Beat-ladder ride** — one continuous take built as 4–6 named physical beats (plunge / skim / swarm / weave / gap / reveal), the mount in named physics modes with mass and inertia, camera position locked for the whole ride (usually rear chase lock). The highest-engagement class. The single time trick inside it is the ride's ONE impact move; the other beats are physics, not effects.
7. **Near-miss gap** — the subject threads a razor-narrow passage, walls blurring inches from the lens, light dipping (plum/indigo, never grey, under 2s for kids) and flooding back on the far side. Compression before a release.
8. **Burst-reveal** — from a tight passage into a vast space in one crane-back: the walls fall away, the whole world opens at once, the subject becomes a speck. (S19's pull-back-to-vast is this move's stationary cousin.)
9. **Rack-focus hold** — a foreground particle or object drifts to the lens and takes focus; the subjects soften beyond it; no time change. The affordable cousin of the slow-mo bubble; use where a ramp would be too much.
10. **Time-snap hold** — the freeze WITHOUT the orbit: camera static, particles hang crisp, expressions locked, then the snap. For scenes whose camera must stay quiet.
11. **Orbit-in-flight** — real-time orbit around the moving subject, the world wheeling behind (crane mode); the lighter cousin of the freeze-and-orbit.
12. **Through-the-ring pass** — the subject flies through an in-world aperture (sound ring, lantern bloom, membrane, seam-glow) and the aperture wipes the frame — a masked reframe whose mask is diegetic.
13. **Curved-reflection cameo** — the riders reflected in the skin of a bubble/droplet/brass dome, ONLY inside a speed-ramp or rack-focus hold on a curved surface. Flat mirrors and puddles stay forbidden (engine rule: reflections break geography); the curved case is proven once (S05A) and stays a cameo, never a shot.
14. **Pinned micro-bridge** — a 4–5s inserted scene between two chained scenes, start frame = predecessor's last frame, end frame = successor's first frame (a Director-ordered end_image exception, as S05B), carrying ONE impact move. Turns a cut into a moment; also a seam solution at hard chain edges.

## The density law (BINDING — supersedes E02's "one impact move per range" practice)

- **At most ONE impact move per scene** (d-120, unchanged), named, bounded, never repeating a neighbor's.
- **Target density: one impact move every 3–4 scenes of runtime** (roughly every 30–45 s of screen time), planned at stage 5 as a per-episode table. E02's original practice (one per 7–10-scene range) is too sparse against the E01 feedback; wall-to-wall effects are worse — the exclamation point needs plain sentences around it.
- **Never in adjacent scenes.** The same move never within 5 scenes. The film's biggest beats get the biggest moves (freeze-and-orbit, burst-reveal, the beat-ladder ride); small reveals get the small ones (rack-focus hold, time-snap hold).
- **Placement classes:** action, journey, reveal and hinge scenes carry them. Lesson scenes, held tableaux and dialogue-stillness scenes carry NONE — for the 7–10 audience the lesson lands in stillness (taste profile), and the contrast is what makes the moves land.
- **Rides:** aim for at least TWO beat-ladder rides per episode, on the biggest travel scenes (a descent, a nerve-run, a chase); a ride may be its own inserted wordless scene (the S05A precedent) rather than a burden on a dialogue scene.
- **Temperament stays the film's:** in E02 the glide law holds — no handheld, no whip inside an impact move; speed is smooth speed.
- **The table lives in `scene_direction.metadata.impact_plan`** (scene → move → what starts/ends it), authored at stage 5 §4d and re-checked at stage 9 when a producer proposes deviating.

## Prompting the move (patterns lifted from the two prompts, translated to the d-201 no-timing style)

- Name it once in the format block: "ONE impact move only: a freeze-and-orbit — real-time fall, then time stops entirely mid-fall, the camera sweeps a smooth half-orbit around the frozen pair, then time snaps back hard and instantly."
- Give the frozen/slowed subject its state: "expressions locked — mid-whoop with arms wide, mid-yell gripping the board, goggles DOWN."
- Make the particles crisp and countable: "every droplet hangs motionless, strung through the foreground like glass beads, each one crisp, individually readable, catching an amber glint."
- Design the sound as contrast: "one whoop cut dead by the freeze into near-silence with a faint crystalline shimmer — then a hard whoosh surge on the snap."
- Positive locks at the end: "One freeze, one orbit, one snap — no other time manipulation, no cuts. Droplets never a smear or blur wash."
- Rides: lock the camera position in its own paragraph ("CAMERA POSITION LOCK — ALWAYS BEHIND"), give the mount three physics modes, write the beat ladder as prose density (d-201: no per-second marks), and let the ride END on the reveal with a held beat of awe.
- Word budget: both source prompts ran long (S05A ≈ 900 words); the beat ladder earns the room. Stay inside 1000.

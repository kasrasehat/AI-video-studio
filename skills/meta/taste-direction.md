# Taste Direction

The creative contract that keeps a film feeling AUTHORED rather than generated. Derived once from the brief at stage 5, stored in `scene_direction.metadata.taste_profile`, and honored by every later stage (storyboards inherit it, clips render it, the grade and score at stage 10 finish it).

## The taste profile

```yaml
design_read: one sentence — what it must FEEL like and why, tied to audience + intent.
dials:            # 1–10, constrain later choices mechanically
  visual_variance:     # how much scenes may differ in composition language
  motion_intensity:    # camera + subject energy; drives cut rhythm at stage 10
  information_density: # how much happens per second; children's ≠ trailer
palette_discipline: how strictly the global palette rules (strict|dominant|loose)
anti_patterns: [what this film must never look/feel like]
```

**Good design reads are specific:** "Bedtime wonder for 7–10s: warm, unhurried, every reveal held long enough to be believed." **Adjective-only reads are rejected:** "modern and clean", "cinematic", "high quality" constrain nothing — send them back to the brief conversation.

## How the dials bind

- High motion + low density ⇒ short kinetic beats, static camera on dialogue.
- Low variance ⇒ few camera setups, repeated compositions ARE the style.
- Density caps dialogue per scene and overlay/caption appetite at stage 10.
- Cut rhythm (stage 10 transition timing) follows motion_intensity, not habit.

## The killer test (apply at stage 5 QA and the stage 10 watch-through)

**"Could this film belong to any story after swapping the dialogue? If yes, the taste direction is too generic."** The palette, movement grammar and pacing should be recognizably THIS film's.

## Audience adaptation (our differentiator — always applied)

The audience field of the brief adjusts everything mechanically: children 7–10 → slower cut rhythm, brighter mid-tones, gentler interrupts, dialogue ≤ 8 words/line, no held darkness over 2s; adult drama → the inverse license. When the brief's audience and its references conflict (Nolan references for a kids' film), surface it — don't average it.

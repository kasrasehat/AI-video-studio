# Video Generation Director — stage 9 (MAIN AGENT doc)

Turn each scene's picked storyboard into a real ≤15s clip. This stage is the pipeline's most expensive per-unit spend by far (10–140 credits/clip) — cost discipline and the regen gate matter more here than anywhere upstream.

**This file is the MAIN AGENT's operating doc (d-137).** Per-clip mechanics live in the producer's chain — `.claude/agents/clip-producer.md` → `prompt-smith.md` → `skills/tech/seedance-prompting` + `skills/tech/higgsfield-mcp` + `skills/meta/consistency-guard.md` — and are deliberately NOT duplicated here. If you are the main agent, do not read those files; if you are the producer, do not read this one.

**Consumes:** `shot_selection`, `storyboard_set` (OPTIONAL — see below), `lock_descriptors`, `asset_manifest`, `scene_list`, `scene_direction` (all read by the PRODUCER as per-scene slices — never by the main agent).
**Produces:** `artifacts/generation_manifest.json` (producer-written, main flips final status) + `artifacts/clip_qc_report.json` (evaluator, optional).
**Gate:** per clip, Director-personal (d-114). **Resume unit:** `clip` (`clip_id` = `S{nn}[A-Z]?_C{nn}`, attempts in `generation_manifest`).

**NO-STORYBOARD PATH (d-029, Director-ordered):** when `checkpoint.py status` shows `storyboard: skipped`, `shot_selection`/`storyboard_set` do not exist — the producer conditions each clip WITHOUT a `start_image`/keyframe. Assemble the reference set instead from: locked character/location sheets (`asset_manifest`, `image_references`, ≤9 images) + the scene's `scene_direction` shot_framing/camera_movement/lighting/blocking/mood text (verbatim into the prompt per the 200–1000 word contract) + `lock_descriptors` identity anchors verbatim. Chaining (d-202) still applies: the predecessor's approved clip's last-3s tail as `video_reference`. This is explicitly a quality experiment — flag drift or degraded consistency at the gate; the Director may order storyboards run retroactively for any scene/range that needs the keyframe anchor.

## Orchestration (Director order d-137 — the four-part split)

**Part 1 — Main agent (you).** Reads THIS skill, `pipeline.yaml`, checkpoint state, and subagent reports — nothing else. Duties:
1. Resume via `checkpoint.py next`; sequence scenes from the previous producer report's NEXT SCENE RECOMMENDATION (chain vs hinge — chained scenes generate IN ORDER, each only after its predecessor is APPROVED; parallelism only across hard-hinge groups; if a predecessor's ending drifted from its board, the successor inherits it — catch that at the predecessor's gate, not the successor's). **[SEAM d-213] The brief carries the boundary class + overlap params from scene_list (`boundary_class_out`, `overlap_from`, `overlap_seconds`): continuous boundaries run OVERLAP GENERATION (the producer starts the successor from the frame `overlap_seconds` before the predecessor's end — the boundary lives INSIDE the clip); re-verify the chain source is the clip that actually precedes in CURRENT timeline order after any insertion; never chain across resolutions; and where two same-world adjacent micro-scenes fit one duration budget, PREFER folding them into ONE multi-shot generation (the cut happens in-model → no seam at all) — propose the fold to the Director before generating.**
   **[d-062 — SAME-FRAMING JOIN LAW (binding, the S17→S18 jump-cut lesson):** when sequencing a chained/new_beat scene, check the predecessor's EXIT framing against the successor's OPENING framing (from the producer report / scene_direction). If both sides of the cut show the same subject at near-the-same shot size and angle, the boundary is a jump-cut trap — two independent generations produce a near-match, never an exact match, and small differences read as a glitch. The brief MUST then require one of: (a) DEFAULT — a changed shot size/angle on the successor's opening (visibly different composition that still absorbs the inherited motion); (b) exact start-frame conditioning, ONLY on explicit Director order (rejected as the standing method at the S18 A/B, d-061); (c) a d-213 fold into one generation, proposed to the Director. Scene_direction that authors a same-framing join ("startable from the chained end frame") predates this law — restage, don't obey. Naturally contrasting boundaries (e.g. cutting out of a frame-filling bloom) need nothing.]**
   **[d-076 - TAIL BY SUBJECT CONTINUITY (binding)]** Decide at every boundary whether the predecessor's last-3s tail rides as a video reference, and STATE the decision in the brief: ATTACH when the successor's opening shot continues the principal subject of the predecessor's closing shot; OMIT when the opening cuts to a different principal subject (faces -> an object with no people, a macro detail -> a wide of something else, an interior -> an exterior of a different thing). Boundary class and location do not decide it - a hinge can continue its subject and a same-location cut can break it. When omitting, brief the opening as SELF-CONTAINED (no previous clip named, no inherited motion, no "the next instant") and let the locked sheets, the shared Style & Mood block, the held axis and the audio design carry continuity. NEVER describe the predecessor's closing frame from memory in a brief - the producer verifies against the actual last frame and has twice caught a wrong description. d-202's tail default is now conditional on this test.
   **[d-081 - STAGING CONTINUITY (binding)]** Sheets lock identity; they do not lock LAYOUT, and prose loses to images (S30 att1 re-staged the whole box-site after S28-S29 established it). Before every brief, run the d-121 PACKING check: if this scene and the next are tagged `PACK WITH` in continuity_notes (or satisfy the eight d-121 conditions — sum ≤15s, ≤~30 words, ≤1 impact move, ≤9 references counting the head tail and every voice with reference media ≤15.2s, prompt ≤1000 words, internal boundary as an in-clip cut, both scenes' laws throughout, same location unless the transition is authored on camera), brief them as ONE clip and state the cost for the full merged duration. Then run the d-115 reference check: does each attached sheet actually show this shot's ANGLE, and does it show the character's STATE in this scene? A missing angle or a wrong state is fixed with a ~100cr derived sheet BEFORE the clip, never with prose and never by hoping the model extrapolates - it will invent a view, and a different one next scene. And where a sheet cannot settle a relationship (which seat is whose, which side of the vehicle a rider occupies), the lock descriptor must state it in words pinned to the world, so d-093 can derive the screen side per camera. For every ABOARD brief, state the d-118 camera case (ahead / behind / right flank / left flank) and the sides it derives - Milo on Gramps's right is the world fact, never a frame label. For every brief, state the staging decision: `STAGING: <element_id>` when the scene CONTINUES an arrangement already established and approved in the same set; `STAGING: none (new arrangement)` when the scene establishes or deliberately re-stages one; `STAGING: none (environment change)` when the location changes completely or there is no fixed set. NOT every scene needs one -- a hinge into a new space, an establishing shot, a transit or an abstract field gets none. When one applies: (A) attach the staging still -- a frame from the approved establishing scene, registered in asset_manifest as a locked environment_plate `<LOC>_<site>_staging` -- @-tagged as the LAYOUT authority with camera/framing explicitly NOT inherited; (B) the element's staging block is pasted verbatim into the prompt; (C) the location plate is @-tagged 'architecture, materials and light only -- not layout', and the staging still wins any conflict. When a re-staging scene is approved, extract its best frame as the next staging still. Independent of the d-076 tail decision. D -- SCREEN LABELS ARE PER-CAMERA; WORLD ANCHORS TRAVEL (d-093, the S36 glider drift). The staging block's `frame-left`/`frame-right` labels belong to the camera that ESTABLISHED the arrangement. The moment a scene moves the camera -- a side-on view, a reverse, a new height or angle -- those labels stop being true and become an instruction that FIGHTS the layout; the screen label wins and the prop walks to the frame edge. Before writing any shot, re-derive every element's screen position from the world layout for THIS camera, and pin each prop three ways: (1) its WORLD anchor, relative to a person or the set and never to the frame ('one body-length behind Milo', 'on the chest's far side'); (2) its DEPTH relative to the subjects, stated separately from any left/right ('further from camera than Milo, partly behind his shoulder'); (3) its APPARENT SIZE, which is what actually encodes distance ('no larger than Milo's head', 'a thumb's width in frame'). Every prop that emits light additionally carries its AIM ('nose toward the chest, lantern on the lid') -- drop the aim and the model re-points it, which silently re-lights the scene, because that prop is often the key. Copying a bare 'frame-right' out of the block into a moved camera is exactly what pulled the Whisper-Wing from its parking spot to the frame edge in S36 att1, lantern turned into the lens.
   **[SEAM d-213 — POSTPONED OPTION]** *WAN VACE Clip Joiner (github.com/stuttlepress/ComfyUI-Wan-VACE-Video-Joiner, MIT, runs on the local RTX 3060 12GB at 720p/16fps with Wan 2.1 VACE 1.3B) can regenerate a bad seam from real frames on both sides at $0 credit cost. The Director has POSTPONED it — do not install or wire it in. It is the designated fallback if a specific seam survives the whole stage-10 escalation ladder and the Director explicitly orders it.*

## Director-inserted scenes (mid-production; precedents S05A/S05B d-117/d-120, S09A d-130 — MAIN AGENT protocol)

The Director may insert new scenes at any point. Scene ids take a letter suffix (`S09A`; the `[A-Z]?` schema extension covers all artifact schemas). In order:
1. **Amend `scene_list.json` AND `scene_direction.json`** — archive both to `history/`, insert full entries at the correct position (same field-completeness standard as original scenes: dialogue with delivery, continuity_notes, negative_notes, transition hints — canon from that moment), bump `total_est_seconds`, validate both.
2. **Log the insertion** as its own decision id (`--decided-by` the Director) before generating anything.
3. **No storyboard page exists** — the scene skips stage 8; the producer builds its references per d-127 from what IS attached (chain frame, character sheets, location plate); never fabricate or borrow a page.
4. **Chain/hinge decision is explicit** in the brief: chain from the neighbor's approved last frame when location/moment continue (S05B, S09A); hinge only when location breaks. State the choice at the gate.
5. Check the FOLLOWING scene still cuts correctly from the new clip's ending; flag mismatches to the Director rather than silently regenerating.
5b. **Re-author BOTH boundary hints — hints are NEIGHBOR-BOUND (d-158, the S05A seam lesson).** Scene N's `transition_out_hint` was written against its ORIGINAL next scene; after an insertion that hint still describes a cut into a scene that is no longer adjacent (S05's "dip through dark and slam" described landing on S06's reveal — applied to the new S05→S05A boundary it punched a blink into a continuous fall). At insertion time, rewrite scene N's hint for the boundary N→inserted and give the inserted scene its own hint for inserted→N+1 (both archived in scene_list). Remember N's already-generated clip ENDS with motion designed for the old neighbor — the inserted scene's opening must absorb that inherited motion (chain from N's true last frame AND continue the motion in the first beat), or the seam will show no matter what the edit does.
5c. **Quality-first neighbor regeneration (d-159):** an insertion changes the CONTEXT of both neighbors — scene N's ending and scene N+1's opening were generated for the old order. Evaluate both seams honestly and PROPOSE regenerating any neighbor clip whose motion/composition no longer serves the new order. **Cost is never a veto when quality is at stake** (Director standing order 2026-07-31): the proposal states the expected quality gain and the price; the Director decides (d-114 unchanged). Silently keeping a degraded seam to save credits is the violation, not the spend.
6. Everything else unchanged: cost announcement + go, per-clip gate (d-114), manifest recording, effects budget (producer enforces — max one impact move, never repeating a neighbor's; the episode's density plan in `scene_direction.metadata.impact_plan` and the vocabulary in `skills/meta/impact-moves.md` are binding, d-085 — a producer who wants to add, move or drop a move brings it to the gate).

## Pitfalls (main-agent scope)

- Invoking the producer before the Director's explicit go on the cost — the go covers the authorized_credits number, nothing more.
- Sequencing a chained scene before its predecessor is APPROVED — the chain input doesn't exist until then.
- Scaling to many scenes before confirming batch cost against balance — video costs 5–10x storyboard cost per unit; surface the arithmetic.
- Treating a skipped evaluator as "no QC happened" — the Director's personal check IS the QC of record (d-036: stills/frame-sweep passes run only on explicit Director request).
- Re-arguing a Director ruling recorded at a previous gate (accepted deviations are rulings, not defects to re-fix).
- Forgetting the d-202/d-203 chaining set on a chained scene: previous clip's last-3s tail as a video reference + the scene's Soul keyframe as a STYLE reference (never start_image, d-203) + reference sheets + storyboard page. Two open Director evaluations run at E02's first chained scenes: keyframe-vs-page adherence, and start_variant none vs last_frame (`start_variant:last_frame` in the brief attaches the predecessor's last frame as start_image) — both variants Director-gated and reported side by side; the Director picks the standing method.
